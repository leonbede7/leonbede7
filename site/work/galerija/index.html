I knew the business
before building the software.

My dad owns a dealership. I grew up around cars, and that interest stayed with me as I started studying programming.

AI caught my attention early. Galerija gave me a concrete place to explore it: vehicle information, customer questions and the daily work of a dealership.

A listing is only one part of that work. Inventory, inquiries, trade-ins and publishing need the same vehicle facts. I shaped the product around a central record so those workflows could connect.

What I contributed

I supplied the business context, defined workflows and acceptance criteria, directed AI-assisted implementation, reviewed behavior and iterated on usability. AI generated much of the implementation. My contribution is the product direction and the work of turning those pieces into a usable system.

Protected operations

Inventory preparation, CRM, trade-ins, reply drafts, pricing context and feed generation. Internal records require controlled access.

The case study describes these capabilities without exposing customer records or application source.

The choices behind
the interface.

The business problem determined the structure. These are the decisions I would start with when explaining the project.

Keep public browsing and operating access separate.

The need. Customers need to explore vehicles. Staff need to change business records and handle customer information.

The decision. Separate the public experience from protected operations, supported by authentication, data-access policies and input validation.

The trade-off. Access rules and testing add work, but an admin screen alone is not an access boundary. The data layer has to enforce the distinction.

Use AI for candidates and drafts.

The need. Vehicle information must be structured, and replies need relevant inquiry and vehicle context.

The decision. Use AI to assist extraction and drafting, with validation and human review before an operating decision.

The trade-off. Keeping a person in the process adds a review step. It also keeps a plausible response from becoming an unchecked business record or outgoing message.

Reuse structured data across workflows.

The need. Inventory, listing content and marketplace feeds refer to the same vehicles.

The decision. Build around shared vehicle data instead of treating each output as a separate copy to maintain.

The trade-off. Incorrect data can affect several outputs. The central record therefore makes review more important, not optional. Reduced re-entry is an intended benefit, not a measured result.

What you can verify.

The public product

The catalog and trade-in entry points can be inspected directly. The screenshots above document their public appearance on the capture date.

The application is proprietary. Descriptions of protected capabilities come from project documentation reviewed privately, rather than a public code audit.

The limits of this case study

I do not yet have measured time savings, sales uplift or lead-conversion results. Those are outcomes to measure, not numbers to infer from a finished interface.

Feed generation does not establish that every marketplace is connected. External onboarding and active distribution are separate dependencies.

What I would measure next

Time from source information to a reviewed listing, the share of extracted fields corrected during review, and time to a first inquiry response. Each needs a defined baseline before I can claim an improvement.
